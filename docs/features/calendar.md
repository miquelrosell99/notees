# Calendar and day pages

A date is a node, not a string (SCHEMA.md "Dates"), and the day page is the date's home in the UI. Two surfaces render the day: the **Calendar**'s Day zoom (the day's time grid — the hourly event register) and the **day page** itself (an ordinary page carrying the note plus its Tasks/Dated/Created sections, so it also appears in the journal feed, search, and everywhere else pages do). Both read the *same* materialized state, never a bespoke query. This page covers both surfaces, quick capture, and the date model; the tasks that surface on days are [tasks.md](tasks.md)'s; the date property's picker is [properties.md](properties.md)'s.

## The Calendar

The sidebar's **Calendar** entry (hide it from Workspace Settings → Sidebar Visibility) opens the calendar — **grid-first**: the month grid with the month's **event blocks**. A **Day / Week / Month / Year / Agenda** ladder in the top bar switches the zoom (the choice is device-local): **Day** is one full-width time-grid column; **Week** is the same grid across the selected day's seven days; **Month** is the block grid (neighbor-month days render dimmed, "+N more" caps a cell and jumps to the day); **Year** is twelve compact months with event dots — a month name jumps to that month; **Agenda** is the days-with-events column (below). Blocks wear the event's **effective color** (its own, else its first class's); a block click opens the event, a **day number opens the day page**, clicking a cell selects the day.

**The calendar is deep-linkable (owner 2026-10-24):** the zoom and the selected day are URL-addressable — `/calendar/<zoom>/<day>`, e.g. `/calendar/week/2026-10-24` (a zoom without a day, or the bare `/calendar`, is legal too). Reloading or pasting such a link boots straight into that zoom and day; zoom changes push history while day picking replaces the current entry (back/forward walks the zooms, day picking never spams history); the tab title names the visible period ("October 2026 — Calendar", "Week of 19 Oct – 25 Oct — Calendar"). A URL zoom always wins; without one the device's `calendarZoom` setting fills the bare path, so the sidebar entry lands where you last left the ladder.

**The time grid (Day/Week, owner 2026-10-24 — the Google register):** an **all-day band** above a constant **1-hour interval grid**. The band stacks **untimed** events as chips in their day's cell and paints **multi-day** events as ONE continuous bar across every covered day column (an event rides the band when it is untimed or crosses days; single-day timed events ride the hour grid). The grid renders all 24 hours per day column (the body scrolls, opening around 07:00) with an hour-label gutter; timed events paint as rounded cards — top = start minutes, height = duration (a timed point without an end = the 1-hour default), the "10:30 – 11:30" range line under the title. **Overlapping events split side by side** (interval-graph clustering). A **red now-line** rides today's column at the current wall-clock minutes. The header row wears the weekday abbreviations and the big day numbers — today gets the highlighted ring; the number **selects the day and opens the day page** (a plain grid-body click no longer selects — it spawns the click-to-create ghost). An eventless day shows the empty grid — the grid is always there.

**Times and ranges (owner 2026-10-24)**: an event's date value may carry a clock time — a block prefixes the label (`14:30 Title`) — and may span a **range** of days, either a `{start, end}` range value (the canonical form) or the legacy `endDate` span metadata older workspaces carry: the range paints a block on **every** covered day (the middle and end days expand client-side, the same compute-on-read register as recurrence). A same-day range with both times reads `14:30–16:00 Title`.

**Create**: a **single click** on an empty hour area spawns an inline **ghost card** — a dashed outline shell positioned like a timed event, with an autofocused **title field** and the span it will write. The clicked hour anchors a 1-hour span (`09:00 – 10:00`); clicking on the **divider between two hours** (a small hit zone around the boundary) anchors the half-hour offset (`09:30 – 10:30`). The ghost's **top and bottom borders drag** to resize, snapping to 30 minutes. **Enter** creates the event right there (the name IS the content; an empty Enter cancels) and opens it; **Escape** or clicking elsewhere dismisses; the ghost's **more button** (or a second click landing on it) opens the detailed modal prefilled with the ghost's day and span. **dblclick** the time grid at an hour opens the event creation modal prefilled with that day AND that hour (a band-cell dblclick prefills the day untimed); **dblclick** a month/year grid cell's empty area opens the same modal prefilled with the day (malformed times and inverted ranges fail loud inline). Creating lands an `event`-classed object with the name as its content and the date written on the system event class's driving date property — and opens it. Press-drag across empty month cells creates the same way (the legacy register: one value per dragged day plus the `endDate` span metadata).

**Drag-and-drop (the Google register)**: drag a block, chip, or span onto another day to move it — plain events rewrite the date preserving the time-of-day (a timed drop on an hour re-anchors the start, preserving the duration); a **recurring** event asks which events to move — **Only this event** (detaches it, like every calendar app), **This and following events** (the series splits), or **All events**. Right-click a block for Open / Delete — deleting a recurring event asks the same three questions.

- **Top bar** — a **Today ‹ [Month] [Year] ›** navigator centered in the bar: the chevrons step the visible period by the zoom's step (a week in Week and Agenda, a month in Month, a year in Year; the period also follows the day you pick in the grid), and the month/year are the panel/popup title buttons — clicking opens the monthly/yearly date-chain pages. The Day zoom's context shows the weekday, a Today marker, the full date in your date-format setting, and the ISO week number.
- **The grid is the agenda** — the event blocks ARE the events list (owner 2026-10-24, the redundancy ruling): the system `event` class and everything extending it (Meeting, Birthday, your subclasses). An event blocks a day when **either** its date property points at that day (a range or an `endDate` span blocks every day of the range), **or** it lives as a block at any level inside that day's page. A node that merely carries a date property without being event-family stays in Dated, not here. Within a day, **timed events list first** (by start time), then untimed alphabetically. Block labels ride the title register: an inline class chip resolves to the class's current name; membership-only events read "Untitled". The events LIST itself (the zoomable, grouped, breadcrumbed agenda) lives in the **Journal's side panel**: the journal is the day-page context that agenda belongs to, and picking a day there scroll-navigates the feed to that day page (creating it if missing).
- **Agenda zoom** (owner 2026-10-24) — the days with events in one continuous, windowed column (Google skips eventless days): a bounded slice around the anchor day grows in both directions as the scroll sentinels cross (the journal feed's pattern), and a day group renders only when the day's events read lists ≥1 event. The group header wears the big day number + the short month/weekday ("24 OCT, SAT"; today's number rides a filled circle) with the red now-line under today's header. Event rows wear the effective-color dot, the Google multi-day time label, and the title: a single-day timed event reads its span (`09:00 – 10:00`), an untimed one "All day", and a multi-day span reads its start time on the first day (`15:00`), "All day" on the middle days, "Until 18:00" (the end time) on the last. A row click opens the event.
- **Repeating events** — an event (or task) whose date carries a **repeat** rule shows on every occurrence day: the time grid, the agenda, and the grid blocks list it (one card/chip/block per series — occurrences are computed, not stored, so there are no copies to clean up). Editing the event edits the whole series — unless you act on ONE occurrence (the calendar's edit/delete on a recurring block): the app asks the Google register — **Only this event** (the occurrence edits into a detached ordinary event; a deletion just removes that day), **This and following events** (the series splits: everything before the day keeps the old series, the day onward carries your edit as a new series), or **All events** (the whole series). The exception state rides the date value's metadata (`exdates` / `until`) — syncs like everything else, no hidden objects. Set or change the rule from the event's **Properties** panel (the ⟳ picker on the date pill — re-picking the date keeps the rule), or clear it back to "Does not repeat". The picker offers None / Daily / Weekly / Weekdays (Mon–Fri) / Monthly / Yearly. A repeating task appears in Scheduled on each occurrence day and never goes Overdue — the series rolls forward instead. **Completing one occurrence completes only that day**: the occurrence-done toggle (the tasks hub's checkbox on the day context, the task's own surfaces) records the date (the occurrence renders struck-through, every other day stays open, reopening removes just that date) — the tasks hub's checkbox, off the day context, still means "the whole task" (the checkbox semantics are [tasks.md](tasks.md)'s).
- **Reviewed days** — a day page carries a **Reviewed** boolean property (set it from the day page's Properties). Reviewed is a property, not a protocol: a plain boolean schema bound to the `day` class (self-healed at a fixed id, `hideWhenEmpty` so the panel stays quiet), written from the day page's Properties, read by the **journal's mini-month navigator and the top-bar calendar popup** as a small tint — a skim shows which daily notes you already went over. The flag is an ordinary boolean property — it syncs like everything else.

## Day pages

A day page is an ordinary page (the journal is the feed of them), and it carries its own chrome:

- **The date header** — the weekday and a Today flag ride a small line above the title; the title itself is the date in your date-format setting, with the day's **ISO week number** as a small flag after it. (The main top bar carries a **‹ Today ›** stepper while a day page is open (right cluster): the chevrons step one day either side and the **bordered Today** button sits between them — stepping materializes the target day page on demand and opens it. The **Reviewed** checkbox is set from the page's Properties.)
- **Three sections**, below the page's own content and collapsed like every system section — hidden entirely when there's nothing to show: **Tasks** (open tasks scheduled for this day, overdue above, with the same done checkbox as the Tasks hub), **Dated** (everything else that references the day — a meeting held that day, a range ending on it), and **Created** (objects created that day — main objects only, blocks are inline scaffolding and never listed — newest first, cards by default with an outline switch). **Month and year pages carry the same Created section** — everything created inside that month/year. The day-page branch is read-model only: a node whose id parses at day precision gets the date header and, below its own content, Tasks / Dated / Created — the open-task partition (the tasks hub's exact write on the checkbox), the day node's existing backlink set minus date-chain sources and tasks, and a `createdAt` range query (main nodes only). All three honor the system-sections lazy contract ([links.md](links.md)) — collapsed by default, nothing loads until the expand, hidden entirely when empty (the Created count is the one structured query, gated the same way unlinked mentions gates itself). Embedded renders (the journal feed) skip both: they already sit on aggregation surfaces.

**Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+T** (or the sidebar's **Today** row) opens today's page from anywhere in the app. Note the browser reservation: Ctrl+Shift+T is "reopen closed tab" in most browsers, so the open-today chord fires only where the browser yields it (and as Cmd+Shift+T on macOS). The command palette answers the date affordances — `is_daily:` prefix, Date Pages section, formatted keywords ([search.md](search.md)) — and the CLI verbs are `notees today` / `notees journal`.

## The date model

- **Recurrence is computed, not stored.** A repeating event is one node whose date value carries a small RRULE-lite rule (`daily`, `weekly`, `weekdays`, `monthly`, `yearly` — additive value metadata, SCHEMA.md "Recurrence"; no op, no wire change). Occurrences are virtual: the day agenda and the month grid list the event on every occurrence day (one card/block per series — never phantom rows), and editing the event edits the series. The rule is authored from the Properties panel's date pill, and a date re-pick keeps it. A repeating task schedules on occurrence days and is never overdue — missed days roll forward to the next occurrence. **Completing an occurrence completes that day, not the series**: the occurrence-done toggle (the tasks hub's checkbox on the day context, the task's own surfaces) records the date in the value's metadata (`completedOccurrences`), the occurrence renders struck-through on its day while every other occurrence stays open, and reopening removes just that date — no nodes are materialized, and the toggle off the occurrence context (the tasks hub) still means "the whole task". Not shipped: the unreviewed-days queue.
- **Events are the calendar's base class; meetings and birthdays are events.** The seeded `event` class is the calendar family root — its single date property (`eventDate`) is the date hook the calendar reads (it accepts a clock time and a range, SCHEMA.md "Datetime": a point `{nodeId[, time]}` or a range `{start, end}` of day slots, full-day = no time). `meeting` extends `event` with a meeting-specific family (its own date plus text location/agenda); `birthday` extends `event` the other way — person-typed: a birthday links TO a person (`birthdayPerson`), the date still rides the event chain, and a person's page lists their birthdays through the ordinary backlinks section. Both are ordinary classed pages — queryable, template-able, mentionable; a date blocks the calendar and lists the object in the day's Dated section like any dated object, and the event family (event + extenders) lists in the events element on its own once an instance carries a date or lives inside a day page. The system `event`/`meeting`/`birthday` classes are plain seeds; existing workspaces materialize the families through the same idempotent ensure as the task family.
- **Local midnight is the only "today".** Every today path (the shortcut, the calendar, the journal, the default-view landing) derives the day from local midnight; the UTC `.slice` pattern is banned by design.

## Quick capture

**Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+N** quick-adds to today's page or the Inbox from anywhere; at narrow widths a floating **+ button** opens the same quick-add modal ([pages.md](pages.md#narrow-screens)).
